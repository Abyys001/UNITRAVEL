CHAPTER 1


Executive summary
Uni Travel wants a website, mobile app and staff panels that find real flight combinations
from Iran to the UK and Europe, take requests, and add cargo. This booklet designs that
platform for the world as it is on 22 October 2026 — which is very different from the world
the brief assumed.

1.1 The reality check
   The example route no longer exists. Turkish Airlines, AJet and Pegasus have no Iran flights before March
   2027; every remaining Iranian airline is under US sanctions, and Iran Air also under UK and EU sanctions. Only a
   few foreign carriers fly into Iran (for example Iraqi Airways via Najaf, SalamAir via Muscat). Travellers are
   crossing overland to Türkiye and Armenia to fly onward.
   Several standard tools do not work for this customer base. Stripe prohibits dealings with people in, resident in
   or citizens of Iran. Twilio, Vonage and SendGrid no longer reach Iran. Google's push service and Google Maps
   are unavailable or prohibited there, and some hosts block Iranian visitors.
   Legal exposure is real and strict. UK sanctions breaches carry strict-liability penalties, and the US has warned
   foreign firms against selling tickets for Iranian airlines. UK travel rules (ATOL) affect when payment may be
   taken.

2.3 Principles
 1    Truth before convenience. Every flight shown carries its source and time; nothing is shown as booked until a
      reservation or ticket record exists; unknown baggage stays unknown.
 2    A request is not a booking. Search results, requests, quotes, reservations, tickets and payments are separate
      objects with separate lifecycles.
 3    The backend is the authority. Roles, permissions, statuses and flight data are decided server-side; clients
      render.
 4    Compliance by design. Screening and policies run before staff act; decisions are recorded; counsel's
      answers become configuration.
 5    Configuration over code. Hubs, gateways, connection rules, workflows, templates and policies are versioned
      data editable in the Developer Panel.
 6    Built for Iranian conditions. No Google dependencies in critical paths, SMS fallback, light pages, honest
      failure during shutdowns.
 7    Simple to operate. A modular monolith on a small number of machines; no microservices or extra
      infrastructure without a demonstrated need.
 8    Provider-agnostic. Suppliers sit behind adapters; switching one off never breaks the product.

4.1 Why this is a first-order design concern

   Payment processors, SMS gateways, hosting and app platforms apply their own restrictions, often stricter than

4.4 Implications for payments
     Stripe — part of the Engineering Kit — prohibits use for dealings with persons located in, resident in, or citizens
     of Iran, and for services originating there DOCUMENTED . Using it for this customer base risks account closure
     and frozen funds. It is parked (Decision D-03).
     Version 1 takes no online payments. Staff record offline payments (bank transfer, office payment) after
     screening the payer; nothing is routed through Iranian banks.
     Whether and when payment may be taken is also an ATOL question (below).


4.5 Travel regulation (ATOL)
UK rules allow a flight seller to operate without its own ATOL as an airline ticket agent only if conditions are met,
including issuing confirmed tickets immediately upon payment and telling the customer the flight is not ATOL-
protected DOCUMENTED . A request model in which money is taken before tickets exist would need Uni Travel's own
ATOL or selling as agent for an ATOL holder, with ATOL Certificates issued at first payment INFERRED . The platform
supports all three modes through atol.mode , defaulting to payment only at ticketing (Chapter 27).


4.6 Cargo and export controls
Summarised from Chapter 9: broad and growing UK prohibitions on goods to Iran, licensing (not exemption) for
humanitarian and medical goods, strict liability, designated intermediaries in Türkiye and the UAE, and an unclear
position for unaccompanied personal effects. All Iran lanes start disabled (Decision D-05).


4.7 Data protection
UK GDPR applies to all processing. Specific points: ICO registration fee; a DPIA before launch; passport data
minimised and encrypted; no inference of ethnicity or religion from names; restricted transfers when personal data
goes to an Iranian SMS provider or any processor outside the UK without adequacy (Chapter 28).

7.3 Normalised seat map model
Seat maps are irregular: row numbers skip, seat letters do not align in columns, and the same aircraft type differs
between airlines. Duffel's own guidance warns against assuming aligned columns or consecutive rows
 DOCUMENTED . The model therefore follows the supplier's structure rather than imposing a grid.


   interface SeatMap {
       segmentRef: string;                             // which segment of the offer
       source: { provider: ProviderId; fetchedAt: string };
       cabins: Cabin[];
   }
   interface Cabin {
       cabinClass: Cabin;                              // ECONOMY, BUSINESS, …
       deck: 0 | 1;                      // main / upper
       wings?: { firstRow: number; lastRow: number };
       rows: Row[];
   }
   interface Row { sections: Section[] }                                 // sections are separated by aisles
   interface Section { elements: Element[] }
   type Element =
       | { type: 'seat'; designator: string;                             // '27B'
           available: boolean; price?: Money;
           characteristics: string[] }                                   // 'WINDOW', 'EXIT_ROW', 'EXTRA_LEGROOM', 'BASSINET',
   …
       | { type: 'empty' | 'exit_row' | 'lavatory' | 'galley' | 'closet' | 'stairs' | 'bassinet' };

    Render rows and sections exactly as supplied; show the seat designator on every seat instead of column

10.5 Role switching on mobile
The brief asks that a customer promoted to administrator sees the admin interface "after the user's permissions
are refreshed". The mechanism is:
 1       An owner or developer changes the user's roles in the Developer Panel.
 2       The backend increments the user's perm_version , writes an audit entry and emits a
         user.permissions_changed event.

 3       The user's next API call carries an access token with the old perm_version . The API responds 401
         PERMISSIONS_CHANGED .

 4       The app silently refreshes its token, calls GET /v1/me , receives the new permission list and rebuilds its
         navigation. If push is available, a silent push triggers the same refresh immediately.
 5       Demotions take effect on the very next request: the API compares the token's perm_version with the current
         value on every call, so an old token cannot keep removed privileges alive.
The details are in Chapter 25.

Itinerary detail
The detail view answers the questions customers phone about:
   Every segment: carrier (marketing and operating), flight number, aircraft if known, terminals, local times with
   the day offset shown when the date changes.
   Every connection: duration, whether it is protected (single ticket) or a self-transfer, whether bags must be
   collected and re-checked, airport or terminal changes, overnight warnings, and a plain-language note that
   transit and entry rules depend on the passport.
   Fare conditions and baggage: shown only when the supplier returned them. Otherwise the field reads "Not
   provided by the airline — our team will confirm". The platform never infers baggage allowances.
   Seat map: offered when the supplier for that offer returns one (Chapter 7); otherwise the customer records a
   seat preference.
   Price freshness: "Live price fetched at 09:38. Prices change; our team re-confirms the fare before reserving."

Submitting the request
1. The customer chooses travellers from saved profiles or adds new ones (names exactly as in passport).
2. The contact number defaults to the verified account number; it can be changed only to another verified
   number.
3. Optional: seat preference, meal or assistance needs (captured as special service requests), best time to call,
   free-text note.
4. The customer confirms two statements: "I understand this is a request, not a booking" and the privacy notice.
5. The server optionally revalidates the price with the supplier (config requests.revalidate_on_submit ), stores an
   immutable offer snapshot, creates the request and returns a reference such as UT-F-8K4Q7M .
6. The confirmation screen repeats the reference, explains what happens next and when to expect a call, and
   sends an SMS with the reference.


11.4 J3 — Manual request
If the engine finds nothing suitable, or the customer prefers to talk to someone, the empty-results screen offers
"Ask our team to find options". The form is pre-filled from the search (route, dates, travellers, preferences) and
adds free-text fields: flexibility, budget, airlines to avoid or prefer, and whether overland travel to a gateway is
acceptable. The request enters the same queue with type MANUAL .


11.5 J4 — Tracking and responding
"My requests" lists every flight and cargo request with its customer-facing status. Inside a request the customer
sees:
   a status timeline with dates;
   a conversation thread with the team (customer-visible messages only; internal notes are never sent to clients);
   quote cards when the team has priced options: each shows the itinerary, total price, what is included, the
   expiry time, and Accept or Decline buttons;
   documents issued by the team (e-ticket receipts, itinerary PDFs, invoices) via short-lived download links;
   actions such as "Provide passport details" when the request is in Action needed.

13.4 Reference numbers
Format: UT-{type}-{code} where type is F (flight), C (cargo) or A (after-sales) and code is six characters of
Crockford Base32 (no I, L, O, U). Example: UT-F-8K4Q7M .
   Codes are random, not sequential, so they leak no volume information; a unique index guarantees no collisions.
   The reference is a lookup handle, not a credential: possession of a reference never grants access. Every lookup
   is checked against ownership or permissions.
   References are read aloud on the phone, so the alphabet avoids ambiguous characters, and search in the
   console is case-insensitive.

15.1 Design principles
   One screen per case. Everything an admin needs to phone a customer — who they are, what they asked for,
   what the engine found, what has happened so far — is on the request detail screen.
   Commands, not fields. Admins never edit a status field. They press the actions the state machine allows for
   that state and their permissions; disabled actions explain why ("Quote needs confirmed baggage on option 2").
   No technical settings. Provider credentials, engine rules and notification templates are in the Developer Panel.
   Admins can read the engine rule that produced a result (to explain it to a customer), not change it.
   Desktop first, mobile capable. Quote building and recording dialogs are desktop features in v1. The mobile
   admin mode covers triage: queue, claim, call, note, message, request information.

    {status_label} , {expires_at_local} ) and validated at save time.

   SMS templates show a live segment counter (GSM-7 vs UCS-2). Persian templates should fit in one or two
   segments: reference, status and a short link, nothing more.
   Links in SMS are short links on Uni Travel's own domain that open the request in the app or website after sign-
   in; they carry no personal data and grant no access on their own.
   Dates are rendered in the recipient's time zone and calendar preference (Jalali for Persian users who chose it).
   Kavenegar-style OTP templates must be pre-approved by the SMS provider; template ids are part of the SMS
   route configuration (Chapter 8).


17.5 Delivery tracking
SMS providers' delivery reports arrive on webhook endpoints ( /v1/webhooks/sms/{provider} ), verified by
signature or shared secret and mapped to delivery rows ( QUEUED , SENT , DELIVERED , FAILED , UNDELIVERABLE ).
Push and email results are recorded the same way. The Developer Panel shows delivery rates per route and alerts
developers when a route's success rate drops below a threshold, so a failing SMS provider is noticed before
customers complain.


17.6 Admin alerts without alert fatigue
New-request alerts go to on-duty admins only (on-duty is a toggle in the console and mobile admin mode).
Outside business hours, new requests generate a single digest email per hour to on-call staff, plus immediate push
for requests flagged urgent (travel within 72 hours).

19.2 Module boundary rules

   BOUNDARY RULES ( ENFORCED IN CI )
    1. A backend module exposes a public interface ( index.ts : service interface, events, DTOs). Other
       modules import only from that file — enforced with ESLint no-restricted-imports / dependency-cruiser
       rules.
    2. A module owns its tables. Other modules never query them directly; they call the owning module's service
       or react to its events.
    3. Cross-module side effects go through domain events written to the outbox (Chapter 26), not direct calls,
       unless the caller needs an answer synchronously.
    4. Supplier SDKs and HTTP clients are imported only inside modules/suppliers/adapters/* .
    5. packages/engine and packages/domain import nothing from apps/* and perform no I/O.

20.4 The request pipeline
Every HTTP call passes through the same chain. Each step is a NestJS building block, so behaviour is consistent
across all modules.

 STEP       NESTJS ELEMENT              WHAT IT DOES

 1          Middleware                  Assigns requestId , starts the trace span, attaches client info (app version, platform,
                                        locale)

 2           AuthGuard (global)         Verifies the access token (signature, expiry, audience). Public routes are explicitly marked
                                         @Public()

 3           PermVersionGuard           Compares the token's perm_version with the cached current value; mismatch → 401
            (global)                    PERMISSIONS_CHANGED

 4           PermissionGuard            Checks @RequirePermission('flight_request.transition') metadata against the
                                        token's permissions

 5           ZodValidationPipe          Validates params, query and body against packages/contracts schemas

 6          Controller → command        Loads the aggregate, checks ownership ( @OwnerOnly resource policies), runs domain logic
            handler

 7          Transaction                 Business rows + status history + audit row + outbox events committed together

 8          Interceptors                Serialises output through the response schema (strips internal fields), maps errors to
                                        problem responses, records metrics

20.5 Concurrency and consistency
   Optimistic locking: aggregates that staff edit ( flight_requests , cargo_requests , quotes ) carry a version
   column. Commands require If-Match ; a stale version returns 409 VERSION_CONFLICT and the console reloads
   the case, so two admins can never silently overwrite each other.

   replayed for retries with the same key and body hash.
   Exactly-once side effects (effectively): side effects are driven from the outbox with deduplication keys, so a
   retried job never sends a second SMS or creates a second timer.
   Read your writes: the API returns the updated aggregate after every command, so clients do not need to re-

CHAPTER 21


Supplier integration framework
Every flight provider sits behind the same adapter interface and speaks the same
normalised model. The engine, the console and the clients never see a supplier's native
format, so providers can be added, swapped or switched off from the Developer Panel.



21.1 Principles
1. Official, contracted interfaces only. No scraping, no undocumented endpoints, no bypassing rate limits or bot
   protection (project rule). An adapter exists only for a provider Uni Travel has a contract or written permission to
   use.
2. Normalise at the edge. Adapters convert native responses into the normalised model immediately; native
   payloads are kept only as compressed, redacted call records for debugging.
3. Capabilities, not assumptions. Each adapter declares what it can do (search, price, seat map, order) per
   environment. The engine asks for capabilities, never for a provider by name.
4. Fail soft. A slow or failing provider degrades results; it never fails the whole search.
5. Compliance first. Carrier and route policies are applied to adapter output before anything else sees it (Chapter
   27).
6. Fakes never reach production. The local fake adapter is refused at start-up if NODE_ENV=production or if the
   provider registry marks the environment as production.

21.7 Deduplication across providers
The same flights can arrive from several providers. Offers are grouped by a flight key — the ordered list of
(marketing carrier, flight number, departure UTC date, origin, destination) per segment, plus cabin.
Within a group the platform keeps the lowest total price; ties go to the provider with the higher priority setting.
Other offers in the group are kept as alternatives, visible to staff.

22.6 Scoring
Each itinerary gets a cost between 0 (best) and 1 (worst) from normalised components:



Uni Travel · Platform Implementation Booklet                                                                                   84
                                                                                                                         v1.0 · 22 Oct 2026



cost = 0.35·price + 0.20·duration + 0.15·layoverFit + 0.20·risk + 0.05·stops + 0.05·timeOfDay

 COMPONENT        DEFINITION (0 = BEST)

  price           (total − min total) ÷ (max total − min total) within the current result set

  duration        (journey time − min) ÷ (max − min)

  layoverFit      0 if every layover is inside the preferred window; otherwise the largest distance from the window ÷ 180 min,
                  capped at 1
  risk            Sum of risk points ÷ 10, capped at 1: separate tickets 3, airport change 3, overnight 2, tight 2, terminal change 1,
                  terminal unknown 1, entry required 1

  stops           Connections ÷ maxStops

  timeOfDay       0.5 for a departure between 00:00 and 05:00 local, 0.5 for an arrival after 00:00 local

Weights are configurable per route. Preferred carriers (configured by operations) subtract up to 0.05. Ties are
broken by price, then duration. Component values are stored with each result, so the console can explain a ranking
("ranked first: cheapest, layover in preferred window, but separate tickets").


22.7 Diversity
Ranking by cost alone tends to return twenty variants of the same idea. The engine therefore:
   keeps only the best of itineraries with identical flights from different providers (deduplication, Chapter 21);
   allows at most three itineraries sharing the same first flight, and three sharing the same last flight;
   guarantees at least one result per construction type (through, self-transfer, gateway) in the Best tab when one
   exists;
   caps the result set at 60 itineraries.

    Self-transfer construction is limited to one hub (two tickets) and to one-way and return trips; multi-city uses

    Entry and visa guidance is advisory and confirmed by staff.

CHAPTER 24


API design
One REST API serves the website, the console and the mobile app. It is versioned, schema-
first and predictable: every input is validated, every error has a code, every write is safe to
retry.



24.1 Conventions

 TOPIC                  RULE

 Base path               /v1 . Breaking changes create /v2 for the affected resources; /v1 stays for at least 6 months after the
                        last mobile release that uses it

 Format                 JSON (UTF-8). Field names camelCase . Timestamps ISO-8601 UTC with Z ; local flight times also carry
                        their IANA zone

 Resource naming        Plural nouns ( /flight-requests ); state changes as commands ( /commands/send-quote )

 Audiences               /v1/* customer and public; /v1/admin/* Admin Panel and mobile admin mode; /v1/dev/* Developer
                        Panel only (separate host)

 Authentication         Authorization: Bearer <access token> for mobile; HTTP-only, Secure , SameSite=Lax cookies for
                        web and console (Chapter 25)

 Validation             Zod schemas from packages/contracts ; unknown fields are rejected on input and stripped on output

 Pagination             Cursor-based: ?limit=25&cursor=… ; response has nextCursor

 Filtering and          Explicit query parameters ( status , assignee , from , to , sort=slaDueAt ); no free-form query language
 sorting

 Concurrency            ETag on aggregates; commands require If-Match (row version); mismatch → 409 VERSION_CONFLICT

 Idempotency            Idempotency-Key header on creates and payment/record commands; replayed for 24 h

 Localisation           Accept-Language: fa or en selects error message and label language; data is never translated server-
                        side except labels

 Rate limits            Per IP and per user, per route group (Chapter 25); 429 with Retry-After

 Documentation          OpenAPI 3.1 generated in CI; published to the Developer Panel; the generated client is the only way apps call
                        the API

Abuse controls
SMS pumping — attackers triggering OTPs to premium or fake numbers to earn termination fees — is the most
common attack on OTP sign-in, and Iranian SMS tariffs rose sharply in 2026 REPORTED . Controls are layered:
1. Destination allowlist of countries (Developer Panel); everything else is refused.
2. Rate limits per number, per IP, per /24 network, per device id and globally per country, in Redis sliding
   windows.
3. Proof-of-work challenge (self-hosted, e.g. ALTCHA PROPOSED ) after thresholds. Google reCAPTCHA and
   hCaptcha are avoided because they are unreliable or contractually problematic for Iranian users (Chapter 8).
4. Conversion monitoring: an alert when the ratio of verified to sent codes for a country or prefix drops below a
   threshold; automatic temporary block of that prefix.
5. Budget circuit breaker: daily SMS spend cap per route; when reached, OTP falls back to email for users who
   have a verified email, and developers are paged.

25.3 Email as an alternative
Email sign-in uses the same OTP mechanism with a 6-digit code (not a magic link, because links break across
devices and are often scanned by mail filters). Email-only accounts can search and save travellers; submitting a
request needs a verified phone when auth.require_verified_phone_for_requests is on, because the team's

25.4 Account linking and recovery
   A user may hold one primary phone, additional phones and one or more emails; each identity is verified
   independently.
   If a customer loses their phone number, they sign in by email and add a new phone (verified by OTP). If they
   have no email, recovery is staff-assisted: the customer calls, an admin verifies identity against previous
   requests and travel documents, and triggers a recovery code to a new number. The action needs two staff
   members and is audited.
   Merging two accounts (same person signed up with phone and with email) is a Developer Panel action with
   step-up; requests move to the surviving account.

CHAPTER 26


Background jobs, events and caching
Everything that is slow, external or time-based runs in the worker. A transactional outbox
guarantees that every business change produces its side effects exactly once in effect, and
Redis keeps the fast paths fast.



26.1 The transactional outbox
When a command changes state, the API writes, in the same database transaction:
1. the business rows (for example the request and its new status);
2. the history row and the audit row;
3. one row per domain event in outbox_events , each with a dedupe_key .
The worker polls outbox_events (with FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED , in batches) every 500 ms, publishes each event
to the matching BullMQ queue, and marks it dispatched. If the API crashes after commit, the event is still there; if
the worker crashes after publishing, the dedupe key stops the consumer acting twice. This replaces fragile "save,
then send" code everywhere.

   RULE
   No module sends an SMS, push, email or supplier write directly from a request handler. Handlers write
   events; the worker performs side effects. The only synchronous external calls from the API are the customer-
   initiated price check and seat-map fetch, which are read-only and time-boxed.

Timers
SLA reminders and deadlines are delayed BullMQ jobs keyed by request id + timer type . When a request

missed removal is harmless.

Jobs that exhaust their retries move to a dead-letter set. The Developer Panel lists them with the error, the attempt
history and a redacted payload; a developer can retry or discard. Every retry is idempotent by design: deliveries
are keyed by (notification_id, channel) , records by their natural keys.

27.3 Matching Persian names
Names of Iranian customers arrive in Latin transliterations that vary widely (Mohammad, Mohammed, Muhammad;
Hosseini, Husseini; -zadeh, -zade). List entries vary too. The matcher therefore works in layers:
 1     Normalise: lower case, strip diacritics and punctuation, unify common affixes and particles (al-, el-, -zadeh/-
       zade, -i/-ee endings), collapse doubled vowels, map frequent variant spellings through a maintained
       equivalence table.
 2     Tokenise and reorder: compare name tokens as sets, so "Ali Reza Hosseini" and "Hosseini Alireza" meet;
       also try joined forms ("Alireza").
 3     Score: combine Jaro–Winkler similarity per token with a phonetic key suitable for transliterated Persian;
       compute an overall score in [0, 1].
 4     Native script: when the customer supplied a Persian-script name and the list entry has a non-Latin name,
       compare in script as well.
 5     Discriminate: use date of birth, nationality and document numbers to raise or lower the score; a different full
       date of birth reduces a name-only match below threshold.
 6     Decide: score ≥ match threshold → MATCH ; between review and match thresholds → POSSIBLE_MATCH ; below
       → CLEAR . Thresholds are configuration, tuned on test sets.
Every screening stores the list versions used, the candidates considered and their scores, so a decision can be
reconstructed later. The approach follows published guidance on screening transliterated names DOCUMENTED ; it
should be validated against a labelled sample before launch (Chapter 32).

32.3 Permission matrix tests
A generated suite reads the permission catalogue and the route table and, for every route and every built-in role,
asserts the expected 2xx , 403 or 404 . Adding a route without a permission declaration fails the build. Ownership
tests create two customers and assert neither can read the other's requests, travellers or documents by id or
reference.


32.4 Benchmark queries
Operations staff maintain a set of benchmark searches (for example Tehran → London, Tabriz → Manchester,
Mashhad → Frankfurt, Yerevan → London) with expected properties: at least one gateway result, no blocked
carriers, self-transfers flagged, layovers inside configured bounds. They run nightly in staging against sandbox
suppliers and recorded fixtures; a change in the results is reviewed, not automatically accepted.


32.5 Compliance matcher evaluation
Before launch, the name matcher is evaluated on a labelled set: true list entries in several transliterations, common
Iranian names that are not on lists, and near misses. Targets PROPOSED : recall ≥ 0.98 on true matches at the
review threshold, and a false-positive rate that keeps the compliance queue workable (agreed with the compliance
officer). Thresholds and the equivalence table are tuned against this set and re-evaluated after each change.


32.6 Test data
   No real customer data outside production. Staging uses synthetic customers with clearly fake passport
   numbers and phone numbers routed to a staging SMS sink or staff test devices.
   Supplier fixtures are scrubbed of names and contact details before being committed.
   The fake supplier adapter produces labelled FAKE offers and is blocked in production (Chapter 21).


32.7 User acceptance
Admins run scripted scenarios in staging before each stage gate: manual request end to end; quote with unknown
baggage (must be blocked); reservation deadline alerts; payment above the four-eyes threshold; compliance hold
and release; promotion to admin on mobile; cargo with a prohibited item; Persian UI walk-through by a native
speaker.

CHAPTER 35


Implementation playbook for Claude Code
How the build should run day to day: the project rules Claude Code must follow, how work
is sliced into epics and stories, and what the first two sprints contain. Paste the rules
section into the repository's CLAUDE.md .



35.1 Rules of engagement

   PROJECT RULES (CARRY INTO CLAUDE.MD)
   1. Stack. Use only the Engineering Kit stack and approved additions (Chapter 18). Never introduce
      ASP.NET/.NET/C#, Python/FastAPI, Flutter, microservices or extra infrastructure without a stated
      requirement and the owner's approval.
   2. Data integrity. Never display fabricated, scraped or unverified flights as options. No scraping, no
      undocumented endpoints, no bypassing authentication, bot detection or rate limits. Fake data only through
      the FAKE adapter, which must stay blocked in production.
   3. Truth model. Keep search result, offer snapshot, request, quote, reservation, ticket and payment separate.
      Never infer baggage or fare conditions. Payment success never implies a booking.
   4. Authority. All permission and state decisions live in the backend. Every protected route declares a
      permission; ownership is checked on every customer lookup.
   5. Compliance. Do not weaken or bypass carrier, party, goods or lane policies in code. Policy changes are
      data, made by authorised staff.
   6. Secrets. Credentials never appear in code, commits, logs, fixtures or reports.
   7. Evidence labels. When reporting findings, label them OBSERVED, DOCUMENTED, INFERRED, UNKNOWN
      or BLOCKED.
   8. Discovery. Discovery tasks (such as the Amadeus panel) are read-only: no form submissions, bookings,
      payments or installs.
   9. Gates. After each stage deliverable, stop, summarise and wait for approval. Never auto-continue into the
      next stage.
  10. Change control. Any deviation from this booklet gets a short ADR in docs/adr/ before code.



35.2 Working method
   Work story by story, each on a short-lived branch, each ending in a PR with tests and a demo note.
   Start each story by writing or updating the Zod contract and the tests for invariants (state machines, engine),
   then implement.
   Prefer small vertical slices (API + console screen + tests) over horizontal layers.
   Keep packages/domain and packages/engine free of framework imports; review boundary-rule violations as
   bugs.
   Update the OpenAPI document, the permission catalogue and the Persian/English message catalogues in the
   same PR as the code.

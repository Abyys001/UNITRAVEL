# ADR-0005 · Identity verification of traveller documents

Status: accepted (owner decisions 4, 7 and 9, 23 Oct 2026, `docs/ADDITIONAL-REQUIREMENTS.md`, Phase B). Extends booklet ch. 11.6, 15.2, 23.4, 25.6, 28.1 and 28.3; ch. 35.1 rule 10 requires this record because the booklet has no verification state, no passport scan and lets any holder of a broad read permission download a document.

Context: the booklet stores passport fields encrypted (ch. 28.3) and lets staff reveal them (audited), but nothing records that a human compared the fields with the document, a customer cannot upload a scan, and `GET /v1/documents/:id/download` accepted `flight_request.read.any`, `cargo_request.read.any` or `compliance.case.read` for every document with no audit entry (ch. 28.1 "Insider misuse: staff browsing customer passports" is mitigated only for the Reveal action).

## Decisions

| # | Decision | Where |
|---|---|---|
| V1 | Each traveller document carries a verification state: `UNVERIFIED → SUBMITTED → VERIFIED \| REJECTED \| CORRECTION_REQUIRED`. Only the customer moves a record to SUBMITTED (explicit confirm after reviewing the values); only staff with `identity.verify` move it out of SUBMITTED. `CORRECTION_REQUIRED` returns to SUBMITTED when the customer resubmits. `REJECTED` also allows a resubmit (a customer can supply a better scan or fix a typo); it differs in wording to the customer and in that the reviewer states the document is not acceptable as it stands.  | `packages/domain/src/identity-verification.ts` |
| V2 | A **material change** sends a VERIFIED record back to SUBMITTED: passport number, given names, surname, date of birth, nationality, issuing country, expiry date, or a replaced scan. Gender, title, relationship and the Persian name are not material. On a record that is not VERIFIED a material change leaves the state unchanged and invalidates the previous confirmation. | `MATERIAL_FIELDS`, `materialChanges` |
| V3 | "Review then confirm": the customer reads back the material values and the scan, and confirms with the hash of what they saw. The server recomputes the hash (HMAC-SHA-256 over the canonical values, keyed from the field-encryption master key, so a low-entropy passport number cannot be brute-forced from the stored hash) and answers 409 if the values moved. `customerConfirmedAt` and the hash are stored on the document. A reviewer decides against the same hash; if the customer changed anything meanwhile the decision answers 409 and the reviewer reloads. | `customers.service.ts`, `identity-verification.service.ts` |
| V4 | `verification_reviews` is insert-only (trigger plus no UPDATE/DELETE grant, the same pattern as `compliance_decisions`): document, traveller, decision, reason code, reason text, reviewer, value hash, scan document, time. The reason text is written for the customer and is the only reviewer text the customer sees; the reviewer identity never leaves staff views. | migration `0004_identity_verification` |
| V5 | `record_tickets` requires every traveller on the request to hold a VERIFIED document (owner decision 4). Quote acceptance and earlier stages are not blocked. The guard lives in the flight state machine; the API answers `IDENTITY_NOT_VERIFIED` (409) and names the travellers. | `flight-request.ts`, `bookings.service.ts` |
| V6 | Passport scans: document type `PASSPORT_SCAN`, JPEG or PNG only (a PDF can carry arbitrary metadata and active content that a lossless stripper cannot vouch for), 10 MB. They live in a **separate bucket** (`<bucket>-identity`) with server-side encryption (SSE-S3, `AES256`) requested on every write and a bucket default; the store rejecting the write is a hard failure, never a silent plaintext fallback. Pipeline: presigned PUT, complete, virus scan, **metadata strip** (below), only then `CLEAN` and available. | `documents.service.ts`, `storage.ts`, `image-sanitise.ts` |
| V7 | Metadata stripping is done by a small in-repo, dependency-free container rewriter, not by decoding and re-encoding. JPEG: keep only the segments needed to decode (SOF, DHT, DQT, DRI, SOS, entropy data, APP0 JFIF, APP14 Adobe, APP2 ICC profile) and drop EXIF/XMP/IPTC (APP1, APP13), comments and every other APPn. PNG: whitelist critical chunks and colour chunks, drop `eXIf`, `tEXt`, `zTXt`, `iTXt`, `tIME` and unknown ancillary chunks. Pixels are untouched, so quality and legibility are exactly the customer's. Why not `sharp`: a native binary dependency in the API image to do something a 150-line parser does losslessly; re-encoding also degrades a document the reviewer must read. The orientation tag lives in EXIF and is dropped, so the viewer shows the scan as stored; the upload screens tell the customer to upload the scan upright. A file that fails to parse is treated as not clean (rejected). | `apps/api/src/modules/documents/image-sanitise.ts` |
| V8 | Downloads: `customer.read.documents` is required for every identity document (`PASSPORT_SCAN`, `ID`) and every staff download of any document is audited through `AuditService` (hash chain): `document.downloaded` with document id, type, owner and the staff member. The three broad read permissions no longer open downloads by themselves; non-identity documents (quote PDFs, receipts, cargo files) stay downloadable by `flight_request.read.any`, `cargo_request.read.any` or `compliance.case.read` but are now audited. A customer keeps 404 for a document that is not theirs. | `documents.controller.ts` |
| V9 | Retention (owner decision 7): `settings.identityScanRetentionDays`, default **unset (UNKNOWN)**. Unset means scans are kept until erased by the customer or by account deletion; a maintenance job deletes the object and marks the document erased only when the value is set (days since upload). The DPIA lists the retention as UNKNOWN and owned by the DPIA owner. Account deletion erases the customer's scans. | `AppSettings`, `identity-retention` job |
| V10 | Address (owner decision 9): optional columns on `customer_profiles` (line 1, line 2, city, postcode, country). No flow requires it in Phase B; invoices (Phase C) and cargo will ask for it when needed. | schema |
| V11 | Second identity (phone or email): the existing `POST /v1/me/identities` starts an OTP challenge and `POST /v1/auth/otp/verify` with the challenge completes it; Phase B adds screens only, plus an inline prompt on the request form when the customer has no verified phone (the API already answers `PHONE_NOT_VERIFIED`). | web, mobile |

## Permissions

| Permission | Gates | Granted to |
|---|---|---|
| `identity.verify` (new) | Verification queue, detail (fields side by side with the scan reference) and the decision | `admin`, `owner` |
| `customer.read.documents` (exists since ch. 10.3, previously used only for Reveal) | Downloading identity documents; Reveal of the passport number | unchanged: `admin`, `cargo_admin`, `compliance_officer`, `owner` |

Rationale: the verification reviewers are the operations admins who already hold `customer.read.documents`, so verification adds one permission and no new role. `compliance_officer` keeps `customer.read.documents` (screening needs it) but not `identity.verify`: compliance screens parties, ops verify identity, and keeping the duties separate lets the owner later grant `identity.verify` to a reviewer role without widening compliance powers. `cargo_admin` keeps its existing `customer.read.documents` grant from table 10.1; it can therefore download a scan, now audited. Whether cargo staff need that is UNKNOWN and left to the owner (candidate for removal in the next access review, ch. 28.1). Four-eyes is not required: the booklet has no rule for identity review, and every decision is a signed, hash-chained audit entry plus an insert-only review row. A reviewer cannot decide on their own traveller (`FORBIDDEN`). Existing databases receive `identity.verify` through migration `0004` (which bumps `perm_version`); new ones through the seed.

## Consequences
- Staff must open the scan to verify; every open is in the audit log. Unaudited broad downloads are gone.
- Orientation of scans is the customer's responsibility (V7). A future switch to decode and re-encode would be a new ADR.
- Customers cannot see a reviewer, an internal note or any reason beyond the code label and the message the reviewer wrote for them.
- Tested by: domain property tests, `apps/api/src/serialisers.spec.ts` (customer verification view), `apps/api/src/routes.spec.ts`, permission matrix, and `apps/api/test/stage8.e2e.ts` (upload scan, confirm, queue, correction, resubmit, verify, material change, `record_tickets` blocked, audited download).
